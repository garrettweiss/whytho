## Priority method

Severity and likelihood use a 1 to 5 scale. Scores are risk-management judgments about foreseeable exposure at promoted launch, not litigation probabilities. Severity 5 means potentially fundamental business or individual harm; 4 significant legal/reputation exposure; 3 material but contained. Likelihood 4 means a triggering condition is already common in the product; 3 foreseeable; 2 contingent. Score 16 to 25 = critical, 10 to 15 = high, 5 to 9 = medium, 1 to 4 = low.

P0 means a prerequisite to broad promotion or to enabling the affected function. P1 means resolve before the initial marketing pilot expands. P2 means scheduled hardening. A control may remain disabled while unrelated work proceeds. Residual scores below are targets **after acceptance tests pass**, not current ratings. Owners are proposed responsibilities, not evidence that counsel has been retained.

## Legal analysis by specialist track

### Privacy and consumer protection

California's privacy-notice obligations under CalOPPA are broader than the CCPA business thresholds. A commercial service collecting Californians' identifying information should not wait to reach enterprise scale before publishing an accurate notice. Disclose data categories, recipients, choices, effective date and tracking practices, then ensure implementation matches. [California Attorney General guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/cybersecurity/making_your_privacy_practices_public.pdf)

CCPA applicability depends on its definitions and thresholds, and consumer rights include access, correction, deletion subject to exceptions, and sale/sharing opt-outs where applicable. Inventory the entity and business processing before promising a particular legal regime. A PostHog integration is not automatically a sale/share, nor is it automatically exempt: contracts, permitted vendor uses and actual disclosures matter. [California Attorney General CCPA guidance, updated August 28, 2026](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa)

Political affiliation, vote-linked behavior and district information are sensitive in ordinary terms, even where a particular statute does not classify political opinion as a special category. Do not claim all US laws define them identically. Treat these as private by design; do not create ad audiences, infer political persuasion or sell individual engagement. General-interest discovery by state/office can work without collecting political affiliation. Source-code review confirms collecting the field, not that it is currently used to target recommendations.

COPPA applies to covered child-directed services and relevant actual-knowledge situations involving under-13 children. The 2025 amended rule's general compliance date was April 22, 2026, already passed at this assessment date. It strengthens consent, retention and security requirements. An age statement alone does not cure knowing collection. The proposed 18+ participation boundary is a product-risk choice, not a claim that US civic speech is legally limited to adults. [FTC rule and effective dates](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm)

No code or narrative reviewed establishes active Stripe document/selfie collection. If introduced, assess biometric consent, purpose, retention, vendor roles and alternatives before launch. Public access from another country alone does not settle GDPR applicability; deliberate EU/UK targeting or monitoring would require a dedicated territorial and political-opinion-data assessment.

### Speech, moderation and elections

Section 230 generally addresses treatment as the publisher of information supplied by another information content provider. The statute defines a content provider to include someone responsible in whole or part for creating or developing information, and contains exceptions including intellectual property and federal criminal law. **Do not assume immunity for WhyTho-generated questions, AI analysis, editorial accusations or WhyTho's own rankings.** Application to a particular AI pipeline is fact-sensitive. [47 U.S.C. § 230](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/html/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5-subchapII-partI-sec230.htm)

Public officials face an actual-malice standard for certain defamatory falsehoods about official conduct: knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard. That is not permission to publish unverified allegations, and accusations about private individuals can have different standards. A question can carry a factual accusation in its premise. Preserve correction requests, source evidence and review decisions; do not let an AI disclaimer substitute for verification. [Supreme Court, New York Times Co. v. Sullivan](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-376/pdf/USREPORTS-376-254.pdf)

Examples of safer product behavior: reject “Why did you steal campaign funds?” without reliable attribution; allow “What controls does your campaign use to prevent misuse of funds?” as an open policy question. When a reliable public proceeding is relevant, attribute the allegation, identify procedural status and seek an answer without asserting guilt. A zero response rate is a description of recorded qualifying questions in a specified period, not a measure of character, voter support or whether the person read an email.

Paid placements, express advocacy, fundraising, political committee status and coordination are separate election-law triggers. A neutral Q&A directory is not automatically a political committee, and a disclaimer alone does not resolve campaign-finance obligations. Review state rules for the actual race as well as federal rules. The current FEC advertising guide itself notes a recent judicial development affecting part of its coverage, reinforcing the need for counsel to review actual campaign arrangements rather than rely on an old template. [FEC public communications](https://www.fec.gov/press/resources-journalists/public-communications/), [FEC advertising guide](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/advertising-and-disclaimers/), [California FPPC advertising requirements](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/learn/campaign-rules/campaign-advertising-requirements-and-restrictions/)

Accessibility review is relevant to public-facing services; the precise Title III application to an online-only service can depend on jurisdiction and facts. A private civic platform is not automatically a state/local government Title II entity. Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the engineering target and test manually, without claiming that a checklist guarantees legal compliance. [DOJ web accessibility guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)

Independent operation does not resolve every public-official account issue. If official users gain power to block constituents or moderate official discussions, assess state action under the actual-authority and exercise-of-authority test, rather than the account's label alone. Avoid promising unrestricted official blocking rights. [Supreme Court, Lindke v. Freed](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-611_ap6c.pdf) Government agencies can also have records duties for official social-media activity; plan export/history support with participating offices. NARA's guidance addresses federal agencies, not an automatic federal retention mandate for every WhyTho record. State/local rules require separate review. [NARA social-media records guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/bulletins/2014/2014-02.html)

Organic promotion still needs truthful endorsements. Founders, employees, family members and compensated partners should make material connections apparent in the endorsement itself when not otherwise clear. Use actual product experiences, distinguish sample questions from real submissions, and do not invent testimonials. Add this check before partner/creator assets leave draft status. [FTC endorsement disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)

### Urgent hosted-media duties

The TAKE IT DOWN Act's platform process has applied since May 19, 2026. WhyTho's public forum makes coverage likely enough to treat as a launch condition pending counsel, even without adult-oriented content. The draft now includes the statutory request fields, expedited removal and known-identical-copy process; publication, staffing and exercises remain outstanding. [FTC compliance guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act), [Public Law 119-12, section 3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm)

Child-safety reporting is a separate regime. Counsel must assess covered provider status, reporting based on actual knowledge of specified apparent offenses and required restricted preservation; the law does not require general scanning. There is no incident finding here. [18 U.S.C. 2258A](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap110-sec2258A.htm) See [content and rights procedure](legal/CONTENT_AND_RIGHTS_DRAFT.md); ordinary two/five-business-day targets cannot govern statutory emergency requests.

### Photos, intellectual property and outreach

Copyright status follows the work and author, not the URL. Federal employee works created within official duties are generally outside US copyright protection, but federal websites can host third-party works; state/local government materials are not covered by the same blanket federal rule. A campaign photographer does not lose copyright because the subject holds office. Publicity, trademark and endorsement questions remain separate. [USAGov government copyright guidance](https://www.usa.gov/government-copyright)

Commons requires individual file review. Capture original creator, source page and specific license; the uploader may not be the creator. Unknown or conflicting provenance stays unapproved. Licensing metadata is evidence, not a warranty from Wikimedia. [Commons reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia)

CC BY-SA 4.0 requires supplied attribution notices, license identification and an indication of changes. ShareAlike applies to adaptations when the license says so, not automatically to the entire independent website. Technical format changes alone are treated differently from creative adaptation under the license. The license does not supply blanket personality, trademark or endorsement rights. Keep crops and derivative files linked to their rights record. [CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)

DMCA safe-harbor eligibility is conditional. For user-stored material, a designated agent, published matching contact, repeat-infringer policy and timely notice workflow are important prerequisites; registration does not license WhyTho's own imported photos. A public process must support valid counter-notices and court-action holds. [US Copyright Office DMCA overview](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca/), [designated-agent directory guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/)

Commercial email rules turn on message purpose, not whether an address is public, the recipient is an official, or the service is free. The FTC describes transactional categories narrowly; acquisition and mixed promotional messages need separate review. Use truthful sender/subject, required identification and postal address, accessible unsubscribe, and timely suppression when commercial rules apply. [FTC CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

X's April 2026 automation rules prohibit unsolicited automated mentions/replies and bulk automated DMs; opt-in plus easy opt-out are required for permitted interactions. A search hit, follow, public handle or someone tagging a politician is not consent to WhyTho automation. The standalone-mention workaround must not be used. AI reply bots have additional approval requirements. [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation), [X developer guidelines](https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines)

X-content processing is subject to the approved use case, display/privacy obligations and changes to the developer contract. Preserve attribution and implement source lifecycle handling; do not treat public availability as an unrestricted republication license. Individual FEC contributor records must not become a commercial acquisition or solicitation list. [X developer policy](https://docs.x.com/developer-terms/policy), [FEC contributor-information restrictions](https://www.fec.gov/updates/sale-or-use-contributor-information/)

## Remediation sequence and evidence required

1. **Before broad promotion:** deploy and verify L01/L02/L05/L07 technical guards, hold automated outbound channels under L06, resolve operator identity, publish accurate privacy/terms/contact mechanisms including L21 urgent media intake, and approve a portrait display policy for unknown legacy assets. Photo coverage cannot be represented as legal clearance.
2. **Before a controlled pilot sends messages:** verify recipient eligibility, channel consent, suppression, deduplication, truthful templates and feedback handling; confirm a monitored moderation/privacy/copyright inbox; apply the content provenance model to pilot questions and images.
3. **Before pilot expansion:** run rights-request, correction, copyright, impersonation, incident and accessibility exercises; audit representative portraits in every source cohort; review metrics/methodology across chatbot, profile, leaderboard, share cards and email; confirm candidate freshness after the election.
4. **Before paid candidate content or new data uses:** obtain election/privacy/IP counsel review of actual creative, contracts, collection, jurisdiction and distribution. Maintain a decision log of the approved scope.

Acceptable evidence for closure: code change reference, passing test or manual scenario, deployed configuration check, responsible owner, policy version/date, and remaining exception list. A draft, plan, unit test alone, or untested migration is not sufficient evidence that live behavior changed.

9. Do WhyTho's precise forum/media functions trigger TAKE IT DOWN Act coverage and child-safety provider duties, and what on-call, notice, copy handling and restricted-preservation procedures will satisfy them?
