## Urgent intimate-image requests

The TAKE IT DOWN Act's platform process took effect May 19, 2026. WhyTho's public forum and media features warrant a coverage determination before promotion; civic subject matter is not an exemption. Publish a conspicuous plain-language route accessible to victims and their authorized representatives, including people without accounts. Assign an on-call owner and backup so weekends do not stop the deadline. These staffing choices are proposed controls. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act)

For a valid written request collect a physical/electronic signature, sufficient identification/location of the depiction, a brief good-faith statement that it was shared without consent, relevant supporting information, and usable contact details. Do not require the requester to upload another copy. Section 3 requires removal as soon as possible and within 48 hours of a valid request, with reasonable efforts to identify and remove known identical copies. Cover qualifying authentic imagery and digital forgeries. Do not defer to ordinary business-day moderation or DMCA counter-notice timelines. [Public Law 119-12, section 3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm)

Record receipt, deadline, content identifiers, scope of copy checks, actions and requester updates. Test access through public object URLs, cached/derived copies and embeds, not only the page. Counsel and security must approve restricted preservation and any reinstatement. ## Content decision rubric

Allow substantive questions, criticism and disagreement. Remove/restrict according to content and harm, not viewpoint or party. Check whether a factual claim is supported, accurately attributed, current and about the right person. Distinguish allegation, finding, acquittal, dismissal and unresolved proceeding. Do not infer misconduct from silence or a low response percentage.

Three reports are a triage signal, not a finding of wrongdoing. Detect coordinated abuse and repeated false reports. Allow a person to flag their own accidental private disclosure. Historical/archived content remains reportable. Provide affected nonusers a path to dispute AI or profile content.

Record outcome and rationale; send only the approved response to the reporter and relevant account. Offer reconsideration and a different reviewer where feasible without claiming an independent appeals team exists. Publish an approved correction linked to the affected content when accuracy requires it. Avoid deleting public history solely to improve a response score.

Authentic published official answers and the original answered-question wording are a permanent civic archive by default. Retain dated clarifications, retractions and changed positions alongside the original, including after departure from office or account closure. Display an author's correction request only when actually made. Suspected compromise warrants urgent review and a proposed temporary stop to further answering; preserve authenticity evidence. Proven fraudulent attribution may be reversibly withdrawn after Garrett's item-specific decision. Narrow legal/sensitive-content exceptions still apply. Account identity detachment requires checking identifying words and media as well as database links.
